# User Survey: Travel Buddy
**Project:** Travel Buddy | **Phase:** Discovery  
**Linked to:** `01_Research_Hypotheses.md`

---

## Purpose

This survey has three jobs:

1. **Validate or challenge the research hypotheses** (H1–H10), starting with the most critical ones
2. **Capture the raw material for persona profiles:** behavior, motivation, expectation, and challenges
3. **Surface unexpected patterns** that generate new research questions for follow-up

---

## Hypothesis Priority: What Needs to Be Answered First

Not all hypotheses carry equal weight. Before distributing the survey, the team should agree on which ones are foundational. If any of these turn out to be false, the product concept itself needs to be revisited.

| Priority | Hypothesis | Why it's critical | Questions that test it |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 🔴 Must validate | H3: Multi-app fragmentation | If travelers are NOT frustrated by using multiple apps, Travel Buddy's unified-platform argument collapses | B1, B2, B3 |
| 🔴 Must validate | H1: Review distrust | If travelers still trust star ratings, there's no case for replacing them with peer-sourced tips | C2, C3 |
| 🔴 Must validate | H4: Non-transactional connection | If travelers don't want local connection (or don't mind paying for it), the Connect pillar loses its differentiator | D1, D2 |
| 🔴 Must validate | H7: Low journaling effort | If travelers are happy to journal manually, zero-friction capture isn't a selling point | E2, E4 |
| 🔴 Must validate | H10: Switching cost | If adoption barriers are low, go-to-market is straightforward. If high, network activation becomes the entire strategy | F1, F3 |
| 🟡 Should validate | H2: Off-the-beaten-path demand | Shapes how the discovery feed is curated and ranked | C1 |
| 🟡 Should validate | H5: Trust as connection barrier | Determines whether safety features are blocking or just nice-to-have | D2 |
| 🟡 Should validate | H8: Privacy-first sharing | Affects default journal settings and social feature design | E3, E5 |
| 🟢 Nice to validate | H6: Locals willing to reciprocate | Important for supply-side strategy, but can be tested in later-stage research | D3 |
| 🟢 Nice to validate | H9: Loose planning style | Refines itinerary feature design but doesn't change whether it's needed | E1 |

## Analysis Framework

### 1. Hypothesis Validation

After collecting responses, each priority hypothesis should be evaluated against a clear pass/fail signal:

| Hypothesis | Signal to look for | Outcome |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| H1: Review distrust | "Local residents' tips" rates at least 1 point higher than "stranger reviews" on C2; majority in C3 say "yes often" or "occasionally" | ✅ / ❌ |
| H3: Multi-app fragmentation | Majority in B2 select 3 or more apps; "I have to use many different apps" appears in top 3 of B4 | ✅ / ❌ |
| H4: Non-transactional connection | Majority in D1 are open to or actively seeking connection; "no money involved" is a top comfort factor in D2 | ✅ / ❌ |
| H7: Low journaling effort | Majority in E4 select "automatic only" or "a couple of minutes"; E2 shows reliance on photo roll | ✅ / ❌ |
| H10: Switching cost | F1 shows interest but F3 shows "too many apps" or "no one I know uses it" as top barriers | ✅ / ❌ |

---

### 2. Persona Profile Dimensions

Group responses by travel style (A5) and motivation (A6) to identify behavioral clusters. For each cluster, these questions provide the persona's core dimensions:

| Persona dimension | Key questions |
|------------------|---------------|
| **Demographics** | A1, A2, A3 (age), A7 (region) |
| **Identity and travel style** | A4 |
| **Motivation (why they travel)** | A5 |
| **Expectation (what a great trip looks like)** | A6 |
| **Current behavior (tools and habits)** | B1, B2, C1, E1, E2, E3 |
| **Frustrations and challenges** | B3, C3, D3 (open text) |
| **Connection behavior** | D1, D2 |
| **Adoption readiness** | F1, F3 |

Aim to build 2–3 distinct personas from these clusters before moving to ideation.

---

### 3. What This Survey Will Not Tell Us

Even with a well-designed survey, certain questions require deeper methods. These gaps should inform the next phase of research:

| Gap | What the survey can't capture | Recommended follow-up |
|-----|------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Depth of frustration** | B4 tells us what frustrates people but not how much it affects actual travel decisions | User interviews (5–8 sessions) |
| **Real connection behavior** | D1–D5 measure intent and recalled behavior, not what actually happens in the moment | Diary study or contextual inquiry during a trip |
| **The "aha" moment** | F1–F4 test concept appeal but can't show when during a trip Travel Buddy would feel indispensable | Prototype testing or experience prototyping |
| **Local supply-side behavior** | The survey targets travelers, not local residents. H6 can only be partially tested here | Separate survey or interviews targeting locals |
| **Offline use patterns** | No question captures whether connectivity is actually a barrier in travelers' destinations | Behavioral analytics or in-trip diary study |
| **Competitive switching triggers** | F3 captures generic barriers but not what specific experience would finally make someone switch | Critical incident interviews: "Tell me about the last time an app let you down" |
