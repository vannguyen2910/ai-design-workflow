# Research Hypotheses — Travel Buddy
**Project:** Travel Buddy | **Phase:** Discovery  

---

## What is a Research Hypothesis?

A hypothesis is a testable assumption about users — grounded in existing evidence — that research will confirm or challenge. Each one follows this format:

> **We believe that** [user group] **experiences** [problem] **because** [assumed reason].  
> **We will know this is true if** [measurable signal from research].

---

## Pillar 1 — Discover

**H1 — Travelers distrust commercial reviews**
> We believe travelers are skeptical of platforms like TripAdvisor because they perceive reviews as fake or commercially biased. We will know this is true if respondents rate "tips from locals/friends" significantly higher than "stranger reviews" on trust.

**H2 — Travelers want authentic, off-the-beaten-path experiences**
> We believe a large traveler segment prioritizes local and non-touristy discovery because mainstream platforms are dominated by paid placements. We will know this is true if "too many ads/commercial listings" ranks as a top frustration and "off the beaten path" is rated important by the majority.

**H3 — Travelers are forced to juggle multiple apps**
> We believe travelers use 3 or more apps per trip because no single platform covers discovery, navigation, and personal tips together. We will know this is true if most respondents report using 3+ tools and identify app fragmentation as a pain point.
>
> *Update (Jun 2026 — P1 interview):* Qualitatively confirmed. P1 uses 8–10 tools per trip and named fragmentation as the single most important problem to fix. The hypothesis understates the pain: the issue is not just the number of apps, but the manual effort of transferring information between them — a systemic friction the survey's multiple-choice format likely underweights. Confidence upgraded from ⚠️ Growing to ✅ Qualitatively confirmed (pending broader validation).

---

## Pillar 2 — Connect

**H4 — Travelers want connection that isn't transactional**
> We believe travelers want to meet locals without a paid booking because platforms like Airbnb Experiences make authentic human connection feel commercial. We will know this is true if respondents value "no monetary exchange" as a key comfort factor for connecting.

**H5 — Trust is the biggest barrier to connecting with strangers**
> We believe travelers are open to connecting with locals in principle but need safety signals first because the unfamiliarity of a new place raises the perceived risk. We will know this is true if "verified identity" and "ability to report/block" are the most selected comfort factors.

**H6 — Travelers are willing to be local guides for others**
> We believe frequent travelers would share tips about their hometown because they understand what it feels like to need local knowledge. We will know this is true if more than 50% say they would probably or definitely act as a local guide.

---

## Pillar 3 — Plan & Share

**H7 — Travelers abandon journaling when it takes too much effort**
> We believe travelers want to document trips but stop when it feels like a chore because journaling competes with the experience itself. We will know this is true if most respondents prefer automatic or one-tap capture over manual journaling.

**H8 — Travelers prefer to share privately, not publicly**
> We believe travelers prefer private-by-default sharing because the pressure of public posts creates anxiety. We will know this is true if "private albums" outranks "Instagram posts" and "private by default" tops the journal feature wishlist.

**H9 — Travelers plan loosely and prefer flexibility in-trip**
> We believe most travelers make a rough plan but stay flexible on arrival because rigid itineraries conflict with the spirit of exploration. We will know this is true if "rough plan but staying flexible" is the most common planning style selected.

---

## Adoption

**H10 — Switching cost is the main reason travelers won't try a new app**
> We believe travelers who find Travel Buddy appealing will still hesitate to adopt it because their social network isn't on it yet and they already have established habits. We will know this is true if "too many apps already" and "no one I know uses it" are the top adoption barriers — even among people who like the concept.

## Research Participants

### Primary — Travelers

These are the core users Travel Buddy is designed for. They have taken at least one leisure trip in the past 12 months, use a smartphone, and rely on at least one digital tool during travel. Age range 18–45 is the priority, though 45–55 shouldn't be excluded. The key is digital fluency, not age.

Within this group, four traveler archetypes map to different relationships with the three pillars:

**The Explorer** travels solo or in a small group, avoids tourist crowds, and is already frustrated by TripAdvisor's commercial bias. Primary validator for H1 (review distrust) and H2 (off-the-beaten-path demand), and the clearest evidence for H3 (fragmentation) — already stitching together Google Maps, Reddit, and Instagram per trip. Typically 25–34, travels 2–4x/year internationally.

**The Cultural Connector** defines a great trip by the people they meet, not just the places they see. Has had at least one meaningful local interaction and would seek it again if it felt safe. Critical for validating H4 and H5 — the Connect pillar hypotheses. Typically 22–35, mix of solo and paired travel.

**The Documenter** captures constantly but has no real system — relies on their camera roll and wishes memories were easier to organize or share. Not a dedicated journaler; they want the outcome without the effort. Key validator for H7 (low journaling effort) and H8 (privacy-first sharing). Often travels with friends or a partner, shares via WhatsApp or Stories rather than public posts.

**The Pragmatic Planner** uses 3–4 apps every trip (P1 interview suggests 8–10 in practice), has a system, and would switch if something was clearly better. Best signal for H10 (switching cost) because they'll articulate exactly what it would take — but also the primary validator for H3 (fragmentation), since their multi-tool workflow makes the pain most visible and most articulable. P1 confirmed this archetype: builds Google Sheets planners, uses AI tools for drafting, manually transfers everything between platforms, and named consolidation as the single change they would make to travel apps today. Typically 25–40, mix of trip types, comfort-oriented international travelers. *Note: Aisha (Explorer) persona reframed after P1 interview to absorb this archetype.*

Aim for at least 2–3 participants from each archetype in interviews, and all four represented in survey responses.

### Secondary — Locals (Supply Side)

People who live in traveler destinations, have genuine local knowledge (2+ years residency), and have some prior experience hosting or helping travelers — Airbnb hosts, expats, couch-surfing participants. Critical for the Connect pillar, which only works if locals participate.

H6 (willingness to act as local guide) is only partially testable through the traveler survey. A separate round of 5–8 local interviews should be planned before wireframing the Local Match feature — not required in the first research phase, but needed before design begins.

### Screener Criteria

**For the survey:** Traveled at least once in the past 12 months for leisure · Uses a smartphone · Used at least one app or online tool before or during that trip.

**For interviews / diary study:** Same as above, plus planning to travel again within 3 months · Has experienced at least one frustration with a current travel app.

**Exclude:** Work-only travelers with no leisure component · People who deliberately avoid all digital tools when traveling.
