## 2.1 Theoretical Grounding

"Stakeholders are all people who have a specific knowledge that the design team needs to build, use, or operate a successful system that fits the business goals" (Dippner, 2022, p. 33, based on Spies, 2014).

Stakeholders are **not** the end users of the product. They are the people within and around the organization — across business, technical, legal, and strategic functions — whose knowledge, decisions, and constraints shape what gets built and how.

For each stakeholder, the design team should understand:
- What **role** does the stakeholder have in the project?
- What do they **know that others don't**?
- What **position** do they hold in the organization?
- What are their **desires, needs, motivations, and goals** for the project?
- How do they see the **long-term goal** of the project?

A stakeholder map visualizes these relationships. The stronger the influence, the closer the stakeholder is placed to the center (Spies, 2014).

## 2.2 Stakeholder Map — Travel Buddy Platform

The stakeholder map is organized by **influence and proximity to the project core** across three rings. Travel Buddy is treated as an early-stage startup building a travel experience platform.

| Ring | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Core** | People who make daily project decisions and directly shape the product |
| **Direct** | People whose domain expertise and deliverables directly impact the product, but don't own decisions |
| **Indirect** | People whose requirements, policies, or resources constrain or enable the project from the outside |

## 2.4 Stakeholder Map Content Areas

As defined by the UX strategy framework (Spies, 2014; Dippner, 2022), the stakeholder map should surface knowledge across six areas. Here is what was captured for Travel Buddy:

| Area | Key Insights Gathered |
|------|-----------------------|
| **Project timeline, roles, milestones, contacts** | Product Owner owns the roadmap; Tech Lead sets sprint milestones; UX Lead responsible for research and prototype delivery |
| **Target group of the project** | Travelers (solo, families, groups) and local hosts — to be defined in detail through user research (Task 3) |
| **Existing customer insights** | No existing users yet (greenfield product); benchmark data from competitor analysis (Task 3 SWOT) |
| **Technical and financial scope** | MVP scope: Onboarding + Dashboard + 3 features; 18-month runway; third-party APIs for maps and booking |
| **Company and brand** | Travel Buddy = authentic, human-first, anti-algorithm; brand voice is warm and curious |
| **Digital strategy, marketing, corporate design** | Mobile-first; social sharing built in; design system to be established from prototype phase (Task 6) |

## 2.5 Stakeholder Tensions Relevant to Design

| Tension | Stakeholders | Design implication |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Speed vs. quality | Product Owner vs. UX Lead | MVP must be scoped tightly — not all features in v1 |
| Monetization vs. user trust | Investor Rep vs. UX Lead | No dark patterns; premium features must be genuinely valuable |
| Content authenticity vs. business partnerships | Marketing vs. Business Dev | Local guide content must not be drowned out by commercial listings |
| Technical feasibility vs. design ambition | Tech Lead vs. UX Lead | Offline mode and real-time matching need to be tested for feasibility early |
