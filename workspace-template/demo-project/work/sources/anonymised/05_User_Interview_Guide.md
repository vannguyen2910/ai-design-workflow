# User Interview Guide — Travel Buddy
**Project:** Travel Buddy | **Phase:** Discovery  
**Linked to:** `01_Research_Hypotheses.md`, `04_User_Survey.md`

---

## Purpose

These interviews go deeper than the survey. Where the survey captures breadth across many respondents, interviews reveal **the why behind the behavior** — the stories, workarounds, and emotions that inform personas and value propositions.

Three conversations, each ~45–60 minutes, structured around lived travel experiences.

---

## What We're Trying to Find Out

1. **Current experience** — How do travelers actually plan, discover, and document trips today?
2. **Challenges** — Where do existing tools and habits break down?
3. **Niche & opportunity** — What unmet need, if surfaced, would make Travel Buddy feel genuinely different?

Each interview tests **all three pillars** (Discover, Connect, Plan & Share) but lets the conversation follow the participant's dominant behavior.

---

## Participant Profiles

Select 3 participants who represent meaningfully different travel behaviors. Avoid recruiting people who are familiar with your concept.

### Participant 1 — The Independent Explorer
**Profile:** Travels solo or in pairs, 3–6 times per year, high emphasis on authentic local experience. Likely frustrated with mainstream platforms (TripAdvisor, Google Maps) because results feel generic or commercialized.  
**Why this person:** Tests H1 (review distrust), H2 (off-the-beaten-path demand), H3 (multi-app fragmentation).  
**Screener signal:** "I prefer to find my own spots rather than follow tourist lists."

---

### Participant 2 — The Social Planner
**Profile:** Travels in groups, moderate frequency (2–4 times/year), heavy on coordination and sharing. Likely uses WhatsApp, Google Docs, or Instagram to organize and share trips.  
**Why this person:** Tests H4 (non-transactional connection), H6 (willingness to guide others), H8 (private sharing preference), H10 (switching cost).  
**Screener signal:** "I'm usually the one who plans the trip for my friend group."

---

### Participant 3 — The Memory Keeper
**Profile:** Travels occasionally (1–3 times/year), strong emotional attachment to trips, frustrated that memories fade or feel scattered across apps. Values documentation but finds current tools too effortful.  
**Why this person:** Tests H7 (low journaling effort), H8 (private-first sharing), H9 (loose planning style).  
**Screener signal:** "I always mean to document my trips but never manage to keep up with it."

---

### Participant 4 — The Pragmatic Planner *(added after P1 interview, Jun 2026)*
**Profile:** Travels 1–3 times/year, international or regional. Builds detailed pre-trip plans (spreadsheets, shortlists, budgets) but adapts significantly once on the ground. Uses 6–10 apps per trip and manually transfers information between them. May use AI tools like ChatGPT or Gemini for itinerary drafting. Budget-conscious; prioritizes food, local culture, and reliable experiences over adventure or spontaneity.  
**Why this person:** Primary validator for H3 (fragmentation) and the key profile behind Trip Planning as the #1 rated feature. P1 confirmed this archetype exists and articulates its pain with high specificity. A second participant in this profile strengthens H3 qualitatively and stress-tests whether P6 (AI import) solves a real workflow gap.  
**Screener signal:** "I spend a lot of time planning my trips across different apps and wish everything was in one place."

## Interview Structure

| Phase | Time | Goal |
|-------|------|------|
| Warm-up | 5 min | Build rapport, set expectations |
| Recent trip walkthrough | 15 min | Surface real behavior (not stated behavior) |
| Pain & challenge deep-dive | 15 min | Understand what breaks down and why |
| Opportunity exploration | 10 min | Probe for latent needs and reactions to concepts |
| Wrap-up | 5 min | Capture anything missed, thank participant |

> **Interviewer note:** Always ask for a *specific recent trip* rather than general habits. Stories reveal far more than generalizations.

---

## Interview Script

### Opening (read aloud or paraphrase)

> "Thank you for taking the time today. I want to understand how you experience travel — the whole process, from getting ideas to coming home. There are no right or wrong answers. I'm not testing you, I'm learning from you.
>
> I'll ask you to think about a specific recent trip. I may ask follow-up questions that seem off-topic — that's intentional.
>
> I'll be taking notes. Is it okay if I also record this for my own reference? The recording stays internal and won't be shared."

---

### Phase 1: Warm-Up (5 min)

- Can you tell me a bit about yourself and how travel fits into your life?
- How often do you travel — and what does a typical trip look like for you?

*Goal: Understand travel identity and frequency without leading.*

---

### Phase 2: Recent Trip Walkthrough (15 min)

> "Let's focus on a specific trip — the most recent one you took that involved some planning. Walk me through it from the very beginning."

**Follow the story. Use these probes as needed:**

**Planning**
- How did you decide where to go?
- What did you use to research the destination?
- How much did you plan before you left, and how much did you figure out on the spot?

**Discovery on the ground**
- Once you were there, how did you find places — restaurants, things to do, hidden spots?
- Was there a moment where a recommendation really worked out? What made it reliable?
- Was there a moment where a recommendation let you down? What happened?

**Connection**
- Did you interact with any locals or other travelers? How did that happen?
- Was there a moment where you wished you could get genuine local advice?

**Documentation**
- How did you capture the trip — photos, notes, anything?
- Did you share anything with others? How?
- Looking back now, do you feel like the trip is well documented, or did things get lost?

*Hypotheses being tested: H1, H2, H3, H7, H9*

---

### Phase 3: Pain & Challenge Deep-Dive (15 min)

> "Now I want to focus on what didn't work — the friction, the workarounds, the moments of frustration."

**Tools & fragmentation**
- Walk me through the apps or tools you used on that trip. How many did you use?
- Was there anything where you thought "this should just be easier"?
- Have you ever used one app and wished it also did something another app does?
- Do you use AI tools like ChatGPT or Gemini when planning trips? If so, what do you use them for — and what happens to those outputs? *(Added after P1 interview — tests whether AI tools are embedded in the workflow and whether their outputs end up stranded)*
- For trips with complicated logistics — like visa requirements or unfamiliar regions — do you tend to join organized tours? What would need to change in an app for you to plan independently instead? *(Added after P1 interview — tests the organized tour vs. independent planning decision point)*

*Tests H3*

**Discovery frustration**
- When you look for places or experiences online, what frustrates you most?
- Do you trust the reviews and ratings you see? Why or why not?
- Have you ever found a great place that wasn't on any list? How did you find it?

*Tests H1, H2*

**Connection friction**
- Have you ever wanted to meet locals or get a local's perspective, but something stopped you? What was it?
- Have you ever used a service like Airbnb Experiences or a guided tour? How did it feel compared to organic interaction?

*Tests H4, H5*

**Memory & documentation**
- Have you ever lost a memory of a trip — a place you visited, a restaurant you loved — because you didn't write it down?
- What makes you stop documenting when you're in the middle of a trip?
- How do you feel about your old trip photos and notes right now?

*Tests H7*

---

### Phase 4: Opportunity Exploration (10 min)

> "I want to explore some ideas with you — not pitching anything, just curious about your reaction."

**Concept reaction questions (pick 2–3 most relevant to this participant):**

1. "Imagine an app where locals — real people who live there — left personal tips instead of star ratings. Would that change how you discover places? What would make you trust those tips?"

2. "What if you could message a local in a city you're visiting — not to pay for a tour, but just to ask for a genuine recommendation? What would need to be true for you to feel comfortable doing that?"

3. "What if a travel app automatically turned your trip into a memory — without you doing anything — by organizing your photos, locations, and even the weather into a private travel journal? How would that feel? What would you worry about?"

4. "If all your past trips were already captured in one place, what would you want to do with them? Share with specific people? Browse yourself? Something else?"

5. "What would need to be true for you to switch to a new travel app — or add one more to your routine? What would make it worth it?"

*Tests H4, H5, H6, H7, H8, H10*

---

### Phase 5: Wrap-Up (5 min)

- Is there anything about how you travel — or what frustrates you about travel apps — that I haven't asked about?
- If you could change one thing about how travel apps work today, what would it be?
- Is there anyone you know who has a very different travel style from you? (potential referral for next participant)

> "Thank you so much. This has been incredibly helpful. Your experience will directly shape what we design."

---

## What to Capture Per Interview

After each session, write up:

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Participant pseudonym | (e.g., P1-Explorer) |
| Dominant pain point | The one thing that kept coming up |
| Most surprising moment | Something you didn't expect |
| Hypotheses confirmed | Which H1–H10 did evidence support? |
| Hypotheses challenged | Which ones looked wrong? |
| Opportunity signal | Any unmet need that surfaced? |
| Direct quotes worth keeping | Verbatim, in quotation marks |

---

## Analysis: Finding the Niche

After all three interviews, look for patterns across participants:

1. **Where do all three feel friction?** — That's the baseline pain, not a niche.
2. **Where does one participant show disproportionate pain?** — That's where the niche lives.
3. **What do they currently use as a workaround?** — Workarounds reveal value propositions: people are already doing the job, just badly.
4. **What did they light up about?** — Energy in an interview is a signal. Note when someone leaned forward, spoke faster, or said "actually, yes."

> **Value proposition test:** If you can finish this sentence with something specific after three interviews, you've found your niche:
> *"Travel Buddy is the only app that lets [specific traveler type] do [specific thing] without [specific cost or friction] — and we know this because [participant] told us they currently [workaround]."*
