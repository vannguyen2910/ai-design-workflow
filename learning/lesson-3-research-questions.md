# Lesson 3: research questions

Working file, not published to the site. Questions the lesson and the AI should be able to answer before Lesson 3 ("Turn Research into Insights You Can Trust") is worked on. Built from the draft `lessons/03-research-you-can-trust/materials/research-you-can-trust-lesson.md` and the four starting questions below.

Excluded: the nine "AI in research" questions already answered in Lesson 2 §5 and Lesson 3 §5.

**Tags.** **L** = the lesson answers it. **AI** = the AI must answer it, so test it on the quote set. **Both** = needs the lesson and a test.

Starting questions (owner's own):

- How can AI help to do the persona, customer journey map, opportunity?
- What existing input can we feed to AI?
- What principles and gates avoid AI mistakes?
- What expected output do we want to see?

---

## A. How AI helps with persona, journey map, opportunity

Extends starting question 1.

1. What does each of the three outputs answer: who (persona), when (journey map), which decision (opportunity)? **L**
2. When do you not need a persona, such as when there is only one clear user group? **L**
3. How many behaviour groups can 24 quotes support before you are inventing a group? **Both**
4. What is the difference between a persona built from quotes and one the AI generates from general knowledge? **L**
5. Does the AI fill a journey map cell from a quote, or from what it assumes about food delivery? **AI**
6. Which journey stages should the designer define first, before the AI suggests missing ones? **Both**
7. What share of journey map cells ends up as assumptions? **AI**
8. Does a pain point from the persona show up again at the journey step that causes it? **AI**
9. Does the AI write an opportunity as a customer problem, or slip a feature in? **AI**
10. How do you rank opportunities by severity, context and reach instead of by how often they are mentioned? **L**
11. Is one person who abandons a task a bigger opportunity than five people mildly annoyed? **Both**
12. What does the AI still not do: decide which opportunity is worth solving? **L**

## B. What input we can feed the AI

Extends starting question 2.

13. Which outputs from Lessons 1 and 2 go in: scope, assumptions, UX audit, competitor analysis, principles, anonymised quotes with IDs, claims and insights? **L**
14. What must be removed before anything goes to the AI (names, company, anything identifying)? **L**
15. Is there consent to put this data into an AI tool, and is the tool approved by the company? **L**
16. Is input with IDs better than input without them? Run the same prompt on both and compare. **AI**
17. Should the AI get the raw transcripts or only the quotes already extracted? Does that change the error rate? **AI**
18. If a claim or assumption is not in the input, will the AI say "unknown", or fill the gap? **AI**
19. Does the order or length of the input change which quotes the AI favours? **AI**
20. How many interviews or quotes are enough before you call something a persona rather than an assumption? **Both**

## C. Principles and gates against AI mistakes

Extends starting question 3.

21. Which gate applies to every line: does it have a quote ID, and does the quote match the original word for word? **L**
22. What counts as an AI error, and how do we count it (wrong quote, invented detail, omitted point, opportunity written as a feature)? **L**
23. What does the AI do with a quote that contradicts the persona? Is the opposing view kept or smoothed over? **AI**
24. Does the AI flatten differences between users into an "average user"? **AI**
25. What does the data not tell us, and is that stated next to every claim? **L**
26. What does the lead see on the one-page summary: decision, claim, evidence, unknowns, dissent, what was checked? **L**
27. Which findings must the designer re-check by hand (high-stakes, small sample, vulnerable group)? **L**
28. When should the AI be left out entirely? **L**
29. Does the AI give the same persona twice from the same input? **AI**
30. When is a process stable enough to package into an agent? (Lesson: run at least twice on two different inputs.) **L**
31. Which steps stay with the designer even inside an agent: Challenge and the quote check? **L**
32. Which insights are verified enough to enter the insight hub? **L**

## D. Expected output

Extends starting question 4.

33. What does a finished persona, journey map and opportunity list look like, and where does each line link back to a quote? **L**
34. What goes into the evidence pack (scope, assumptions, findings, quotes, claims)? **L**
35. What does an insight hub entry contain: ID, quote IDs, date, evidence strength, tag, owner? **L**
36. What does the designer hand to the lead, and what decision does it support? **L**
37. What changes in the assumption list after this lesson: confirmed, not confirmed, unknown? **L**
38. What is the output of the practice: persona, journey map, opportunity, a one-page summary and a hub entry? **L**

## E. Time, tools and measurement

Not in the starting four, but the lesson raises them.

39. How much time does the AI really save across the whole process, including checking? The only number the lesson cites covers one step. **Both**
40. How do you pick an AI tool: data handling, traceable sources, can say unknown, company-approved, exportable? **L**
41. How do you find an existing insight in the hub before running new research? **L**
42. How do you keep a hub entry current when new evidence arrives instead of adding a duplicate? **L**
