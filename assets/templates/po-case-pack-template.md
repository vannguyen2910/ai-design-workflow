# Stand-in PO Case Pack (template)

**Used in:** the "Discover the problem" lesson, as the **fallback** for students who have no access to a real PO, and as practice material. Students normally rehearse with a fact sheet about their own stakeholder; this pack is the ready-made alternative.
**Why it exists:** students need a real conversation with a business stakeholder to practise the partner moves. A person plays the PO, from this pack, so the answers are consistent and never come from the AI. (The AI prepares questions and drafts notes; it does not play the PO.)
**Who plays the PO:** the instructor, or a peer in pairs. In pairs, each person plays the PO once, for the other's case.

## 1. For the student (shared before the meeting)
- **Product / company (public facts only):** [name, what it does, who it serves]
- **The business question, as the business would say it:** [one or two sentences, e.g. "We want more new customers to finish setup."]
- **What exists:** [a short PRD or brief, or "no PRD yet"; links to public material]
- **Time for the meeting:** [15 minutes]

## 2. For the PO player only (do not share)
**Role:** [Head of Product / product owner at the company]. **Manner:** [busy, friendly, wants to move fast, but will give detail when asked a good question].

| The student asks about… | The PO knows (say only when asked) |
|---|---|
| The outcome | [The result the business wants, e.g. "raise 30-day activation from 38% to 45% this quarter"] |
| The metric and baseline | [How it is measured, who owns the dashboard, the current number] |
| Users | [Who the key segment is, one thing the business believes about them] |
| Constraints | [Budget, deadline, platform, legal or brand limits] |
| What was tried | [One earlier attempt and what happened] |
| What would make us stop | [Rollback trigger] |
| Assumptions the business holds that are weak | [One belief with little evidence, which a good question can expose] |

**Rules for the PO player**
1. Answer **only from this pack**. If asked something not in it, say "we haven't decided that" or "I don't know". Never invent a number.
2. Reward good questions: give a detail only when the student asks about it, not unprompted.
3. Do not solve the problem for the student. If they jump to screens, ask "what would success look like?"
4. At the end, ask the student to read their draft scope back. Say what you would change. Agree, or agree with changes, or say "not yet" (this is the **agreement status**).

## 3. After the meeting (student)
- Write notes the same day. Give the AI **your notes** (not the pack) and ask for a draft scope, then check the draft against your notes.
- Record every number the PO gave. Mark anything you could not confirm as **assumed**.
- Share the draft scope back (this is the first use of the live hub link) and record the agreement status: **agreed**, **pending** or **not available**.

## 4. Quality checklist for a case pack
- [ ] Uses public facts only, or fictional ones clearly marked as fictional
- [ ] Contains at least one weak assumption a good question can expose
- [ ] Contains numbers (baseline and target) the student must ask for to learn
- [ ] Small enough to explore in a 15-minute conversation
- [ ] Gives the PO player clear "I don't know" boundaries

## 5. Short example (fictional)
- **Company:** a meal-kit subscription app (fictional). **Business question:** "Too many new customers pause after the first box."
- **PO knows:** 30-day retention 41%, target 50% this quarter, owned by the growth team's dashboard; the first-box delivery date picker confuses people (anecdotal); a discount email was tried last quarter with little effect; the weak assumption: "people leave because of price" (no evidence).
