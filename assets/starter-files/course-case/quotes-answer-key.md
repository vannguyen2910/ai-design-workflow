## What this shows
- **The team's belief ("price is the main reason") is contradicted.** Only 2 of 24 mention price, one kindly. One interviewee says cooking time bothers them more than price (I02-03).
- The strongest opportunities are about the **first-box experience**: knowing the date is final (or being able to change it), and pausing with a clear cut-off.

## Traps to watch for
1. **R10** (rude courier) is about delivery, so an AI may file it under theme A. It is a courier problem. A good answer puts it in "unclassified" or a separate "delivery logistics" note.
2. **R09** is noise; a good answer does not make a theme of it.
3. **Paraphrased quotes.** A typical failure is a quote that is shortened or reworded. Students must check every cited quote against `quotes.md`, word for word.
4. **Counting versus weighing.** C has five quotes but is less severe than A and B; D has two and should be labelled weak.
5. **Sample limits.** 24 quotes, one app, two interviewees: themes are hypotheses, not conclusions.

## Examples of good opportunity statements
- Customers need to know a delivery date is final before they commit, or be able to change it (A: R01, F02, I02-01).
- Customers need an obvious way to pause or skip a week, with the cut-off shown where they pause (B: R04, F04, I01-02).
- Customers need honest cooking times, so the first recipe does not feel like failure (C: R05, I01-03).
