|---|---|---|---|

## What this shows
- **The team's belief ("price is the main reason") is contradicted.** Only 2 of 24 notes mention price, one kindly. One interviewee says waiting time bothers them more than price (I02-03).
- The strongest opportunities are about the **first-order experience**: knowing the time slot is final (or being able to change it), and cancelling with a clear cut-off.

## Traps to watch for
1. **R10** (wrong and cold food) is about delivery, so an AI may file it under theme A or C. It is a restaurant problem. A good answer puts it in "unclassified" or a separate "restaurant quality" note.
2. **R09** is noise; a good answer does not make a theme of it.
3. **Wrong or reworded sources.** A typical failure is an insight that cites a source ID whose note says something different, or a note that is shortened or reworded. Students must open every cited source ID in `source-notes.md` and check it says what the insight says.
4. **Counting versus weighing.** C has five notes but is less severe than A and B; D has two and should be labelled weak.
5. **Sample limits.** 24 notes, one app, two interviewees: themes are hypotheses, not conclusions.

## Examples of good insights
- Customers need to know a delivery time slot is final before they commit, or be able to change it (A: R01, F02, I02-01).
- Customers need an obvious way to cancel an order, with the cut-off shown where they cancel (B: R04, F04, I01-02).
- Customers need honest delivery estimates, so the first order does not feel like failure (C: R05, I01-03).

## Check on the AI
- Does every source ID exist in `source-notes.md`, and does that note say what the insight says?
- Did it label D as weak?
- Did it say what the sample cannot tell us, and test the "price" belief honestly?
